Practice

Studio Lintel is five people in a converted joinery workshop. We take on a small number of projects each year, so the people who draw a building also see it built.

Most of our work is for families improving a house they already love: Queenslanders that need their verandahs back, post-war houses with dark middles, cottages on lots too small for the brief. We also design a handful of new houses and the occasional small public building.

We work from first sketch to handover — site study, design, documentation, approvals and contract administration — and we stay on call once you move in.

Approach

Five principles we test every drawing against.

  1. 1

    Start with the sun.

    Every project begins with a sun study of the site: where winter light reaches, where the western sun needs stopping. The plan follows from that, not the other way round.

  2. 2

    Keep the lintel low.

    Door heads and window heads set close to the eye make rooms feel sheltered, so a single tall opening feels generous. We draw the heights of openings as carefully as their widths.

  3. 3

    Build less, better.

    Most briefs get smaller in our studio. Fewer square metres, built from better materials, usually cost less to run and are more pleasant to live in.

  4. 4

    Let the house breathe.

    A Brisbane house should work with the windows open for much of the year. Cross-ventilation, deep eaves and shaded outdoor rooms come before the air-conditioning plan.

  5. 5

    Leave room for change.

    Families grow and shrink. We plan structure and services so a house can be adapted later without starting again.
