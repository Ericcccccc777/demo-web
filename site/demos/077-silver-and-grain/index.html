    ▸ 01 — Approach

    Slow film, quick feet, and nobody standing around waiting for photos.

    1. 01A

      Film first

      Around sixteen rolls on a full day: 35mm for everything that moves, medium format for portraits. A small digital camera comes along for the darkest corners of the reception.

    2. 02A

      Fewer, better frames

      A roll only holds so many, so I watch before I press. Expect about 500 finished images from a full day — enough to tell it properly, few enough that you'll actually look at them.

    3. 03A

      Developed by hand

      Black-and-white rolls are developed in my darkroom in Katoomba; colour goes to a lab in Sydney. I scan and correct every frame myself.

    4. 04A

      Weather is welcome

      Fog on the escarpment is one of my favourite things to photograph. We'll still plan a wet-weather spot, and I bring clear umbrellas.

    Questions couples ask

    Do we get the negatives?

    Yes. Once your scans are delivered, your negatives are sleeved, labelled and posted to you. They're yours to keep.

    How long until we see the photos?

    A sneak peek of about thirty images within two weeks, then the full set in six to eight weeks. Film takes a little longer — that's part of it.

    Can we have colour and black-and-white?

    Most weddings get both. I carry cameras loaded with each, so your black-and-white frames are true black-and-white film, not converted.

    What if it rains or the fog rolls in?

    We work with it. Mist in the valleys is some of the best light the mountains have, and we'll always have an undercover plan.
