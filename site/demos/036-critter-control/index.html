Meet the cast

The usual suspects

Six Top End troublemakers. Flip a mugshot for the rap sheet: what it’s known for, the signs to look for, and our move.

Swipe the line-up →

  1. — 12 mm

    Nº 01 · Termite

    The giant northern termite

    aka Mastotermes darwiniensis, the Top End’s heavyweight

    Rap sheet · Nº 01

    Known for
    Eating timber from the inside out: stumps, frames, fences, even living trees.
    Signs
    Mud tubes on stumps and piers, hollow-sounding timber, blistered paint, mud packed into cracks.
    Our move
    Inspection first. Don’t spray or break open the workings; it only sends them somewhere else in the house.
  2. — 13 mm

    Nº 02 · Cockroach

    The German cockroach

    aka the one behind your fridge

    Rap sheet · Nº 02

    Known for
    Breeding fast in warm, damp kitchens and hitching rides in boxes and appliances.
    Signs
    Pepper-like droppings, a musty smell, egg cases tucked behind fridges and under sinks.
    Our move
    Targeted gel baits where they hide, not a cloud of spray, plus a follow-up.
  3. — 150 mm leg span

    Nº 03 · Huntsman

    The huntsman

    aka the dashboard surprise

    Rap sheet · Nº 03

    Known for
    Being large, fast and flat enough to live behind picture frames and sun visors.
    Signs
    You, screaming. They’re shy hunters that eat other insects and rarely bother people.
    Our move
    Relocate outside where we can; treat eaves and frames if there are lots.
  4. — 10 mm

    Nº 04 · Redback

    The redback

    aka the one in the letterbox

    Rap sheet · Nº 04

    Known for
    Messy, tangled webs in dry, sheltered spots: under outdoor chairs, in sheds and letterboxes.
    Signs
    Untidy webs low to the ground with small round egg sacs. The bite is venomous: get medical advice promptly.
    Our move
    Treat hiding spots around the house; gloves on for yard work.
  5. — 180 mm + tail

    Nº 05 · Roof rat

    The roof rat

    aka the 2am ceiling tap-dancer

    Rap sheet · Nº 05

    Known for
    Climbing palms and power lines into roof voids, then chewing whatever is up there.
    Signs
    Scratching at night, droppings in the roof, gnawed cables, greasy rub marks along beams.
    Our move
    Find and seal the way in, then stations or traps over three visits.
  6. — 5 mm

    Nº 06 · Mosquito

    The mozzie

    aka the wet-season whine

    Rap sheet · Nº 06

    Known for
    Breeding in any still water after rain, from pot-plant saucers to blocked gutters.
    Signs
    Wrigglers in standing water; bites at dusk and dawn. Some mosquitoes carry disease, so cover up.
    Our move
    Remove breeding sites first, then a barrier treatment on shady foliage.

General information only — for bites, stings or health worries, talk to a doctor or pharmacist.

The Top End survival guide

Eight things you can do this week so the critters have less to come for.

  1. 1Starve them of water

    Fix dripping taps and air-con drains. Cockroaches can go longer without food than without water.

  2. 2Don’t feed the monsters

    Seal food, wipe benches at night, and take the bin out before it gets lively.

  3. 3Mind the gap

    Keep mulch, soil and timber off the slab and clear of weep holes, so termite activity stays visible.

  4. 4Tip it out

    In the Wet, empty pot-plant saucers, buckets and tarps weekly. Wrigglers need only a splash.

  5. 5Look up

    Scratching in the ceiling at night? Trim palms and branches back from the roofline.

  6. 6Gloves in the shed

    Redbacks love dry, sheltered corners. Gloves for the shed, the letterbox and under outdoor chairs.

  7. 7Leave the workings alone

    Found termites? Don’t spray or break open the mud. Call for an inspection and leave them be until then.

  8. 8Book the yearly check

    In the Top End, most homes are worth a termite inspection at least once a year.
