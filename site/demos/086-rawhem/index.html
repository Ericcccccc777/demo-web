    Atelier

    Brunswick, Melbourne VIC

    One long table, two industrial machines and one rule: cut once, finish once.

    Patterns are drafted flat so they nest on the cloth like a puzzle. Offcuts become pocket bags and hanging loops. Hems are left raw on purpose — they soften into a short fringe over the first few washes, then stop at a line of stitching.

    1. 01DraftFlat patterns on brown paper, pinned to the wall.
    2. 02NestPieces laid edge to edge across the full width.
    3. 03CutBy hand, one layer at a time, on the long table.
    4. 04Leave itOne stitch line in from every raw edge. Nothing more.

    Care

    Raw edges are a finish, not a fault. Treat them gently for the first few washes and they settle into a soft fringe.

    • Cold wash30° max, inside out, with darks.
    • Hang to airNo tumble dryer — heat shrinks drill.
    • Dry flatThe Bias Slip and knit rib, always.
    • Don't trimCutting the fringe restarts the fray.
    • Bring it backSplit seams re-stitched at the atelier.
    Will the raw hem keep fraying?

    No. It softens to a 3–5 mm fringe over the first few washes, then stops at a line of stitching set in from every edge. If it ever runs past that line, bring it in and we'll re-stitch it.

    Can you shorten a piece?

    Yes — raw hems make it simple. At the atelier we mark and cut to your length while you wait. Sample policy: free on SS27 pieces.

    Returns and exchanges?

    Sample policy for this demo: exchange or refund within 30 days if unworn with the tag on. Faulty items are always covered under Australian Consumer Law.

    Where is it made?

    Cut and sewn at the atelier in Brunswick from end-of-roll and deadstock cloth, so some looks stop when the bolt runs out.
