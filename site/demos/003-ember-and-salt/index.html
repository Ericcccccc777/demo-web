After the menu: the room ↓

The room

Twenty-eight seats, all turned towards one fire.

A former print shop on a Surry Hills back lane, with the hearth built into the old loading-dock wall. Ten counter stools face the fire directly, so you can watch each course leave the coals.

  • 10Counter stools, facing the hearth — best for one or two
  • 5Tables for two along the brick wall
  • 1Long table under the skylight for parties of seven or eight

The fire

Ironbark for heat, red gum for colour, vine cuttings for perfume.

There is no gas line in this kitchen. Every course is cooked on, in, over or beside one brick hearth, and the menu moves with the fire through the night.

  1. 11:00 am

    Lit

    Kindling and split ironbark. The bricks take three hours to come up to heat.

  2. 2:00 pm

    Bread coals

    The hearth floor at its hottest. Flatbreads blister in under a minute.

  3. 5:00 pm

    Service fire

    Red gum goes on for a steadier coal. The first oysters go over at 5:30.

  4. 8:00 pm

    Second wind

    Fresh ironbark for the late seatings; the duck roasts at the edge of the flames.

  5. 11:30 pm

    Banked

    Ash raked over the coals. Tomorrow’s leeks go into the embers overnight.

“We don’t cook to a clock. We cook to the colour of the coals.”— written on the kitchen whiteboard

Before you come

Dietary notes, hours and how to find us.

Dietary requirements

With 48 hours’ notice we cook vegetarian, pescatarian, gluten-free and dairy-free versions of every course.

  • Tell us in your booking notes, or call the restaurant.
  • We can’t safely cook for severe nut or shellfish allergies — our hearth, benches and tools are shared.
  • A fully vegan menu isn’t possible on this fire, and we’d rather tell you now.
