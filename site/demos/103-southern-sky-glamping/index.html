Stargazing

Find the Southern Cross

Click or tap the stars in order — Tab and Enter work too. Draw the Cross and its two Pointers, and the sky will show you which way is south.

Step 1 of 6Start at the top of the Cross: Gacrux, a slightly orange star.

Using the Cross to find south

  1. Find the long axis of the Cross, from Gacrux at the top to Acrux at the foot.
  2. Extend that line about four and a half times its own length.
  3. Draw a line at right angles through the middle of the two Pointers.
  4. Where the lines meet is roughly the South Celestial Pole — the point the whole sky turns around.
  5. Drop straight down to the horizon. You’re facing (roughly) south.

From the Grampians the Cross never sets — it wheels around the pole all night. General astronomy only: for navigation, carry a map and compass.
