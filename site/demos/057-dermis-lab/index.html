0 – 100 µm

Epidermis

The surface you see and touch — on your face, about as thin as a sheet of paper. New cells form at the base and take around four weeks to rise, flatten and shed.

  • 1Stratum corneum. Flattened, dead surface cells that form the barrier.
  • 2Keratinocytes. The working cells of the epidermis, maturing as they move up.
  • 3Melanocyte. Makes melanin, the pigment behind tanning and dark spots.

100 – 2,000 µm

Dermis

The living scaffold underneath: collagen and elastin fibres, blood vessels, nerves, hair follicles and glands. It gives skin its strength and spring.

  • 4Capillary loops. Tiny vessels that feed the epidermis above.
  • 5Collagen and elastin. Bundles of fibres made by fibroblast cells.
  • 6Hair follicle. With its oil-producing sebaceous gland (7).
  • 8Sweat gland. A coiled tube with a duct that spirals up to a pore.

2,000 µm and deeper

Subcutaneous layer

Soft lobules of fat cells divided by fibrous walls, with the larger vessels that supply the skin above. It cushions, insulates and stores energy.

  • 9Adipocytes. Fat cells, each mostly one large droplet.
  • 10Larger blood vessels. Feeding the networks in the dermis.

      Questions we hear a lot

      Do I need a consultation first?

      For peels and microneedling, yes. It’s where we check your skin, medications and history — and it’s credited to your first treatment.

      Does microneedling hurt?

      We apply numbing cream first. Most people describe pressure and a scratchy feeling. You can ask us to pause at any time.

      Can I have treatments while pregnant or breastfeeding?

      Some treatments aren’t suitable. Tell us at your consultation and we’ll suggest what is.

      I use prescription acne medication. Is that a problem?

      Some acne medications make skin more fragile. Tell us before any peel or microneedling — we may need to wait, or suggest you check with your GP.

      When should I see a doctor instead?

      A changing mole, a sore that doesn’t heal, a sudden rash or signs of infection — please see your GP. We’re a skin clinic, not a medical practice.
